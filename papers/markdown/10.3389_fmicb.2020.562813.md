Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons: Sources, Toxicity, and Remediation Approaches  
Avani Bharatkumar Patel, Shabnam Shaikh, Kunal R. Jain, Chirayu Desai*, and Datta Madamwar*

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are widespread across the globe, primarily due to long-term anthropogenic sources of pollution. The inherent properties of PAHs, such as their heterocyclic aromatic ring structures, hydrophobicity, and thermostability, make them recalcitrant and highly persistent in the environment. PAH pollutants are highly toxic, mutagenic, carcinogenic, teratogenic, and immunotoxicogenic to various life forms. This review discusses the primary sources of PAH emissions, routes of exposure, and their toxic effects, particularly on humans. It summarizes various remediation approaches, including physical and chemical methods such as membrane filtration, soil washing, adsorption, electrokinetic, thermal, oxidation, and photocatalytic treatments. 

The review emphasizes eco-friendly biological treatment solutions for PAH remediation, including microbial approaches with bacteria, archaea, fungi, algae, and co-cultures. Both in situ and ex situ biological treatments, such as land farming, biostimulation, bioaugmentation, phytoremediation, bioreactors, and vermiremediation, are discussed in detail. Additionally, factors affecting and limiting PAH bioremediation are reviewed. The paper also provides an overview of emerging technologies employing combinatorial treatment methods and highlights newer concepts that generate value-added by-products during PAH remediation.

**Keywords:** toxicity, bioavailability, biostimulation, microbial remediation, integrated technologies

### INTRODUCTION

Rapid industrialization and urbanization have generated numerous anthropogenic activities, which contribute to the environmental deposition of pollutants, including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) (Mojiri et al., 2019). Due to their physicochemical properties, PAHs persist in the environment and display a wide range of toxic effects. PAHs are ubiquitous and found equally among aquatic, terrestrial ecosystems, and the atmosphere (Adeniji et al., 2019). Their deposition is accelerated in soil and sediments due to their high hydrophobicity and low aqueous solubility. PAHs are strongly adsorbed onto soil particles, making soils a significant sink for these pollutants (Lu et al., 2011; Kuppusamy et al., 2017). PAH pollution in soils can be classified into three categories:  
- **Unpolluted:** PPAH < 200 ng·g⁻¹  
- **Weakly polluted:** PAH 200–600 ng·g⁻¹  
- **Heavily polluted:** PAH > 1,000 ng·g⁻¹ (Wu et al., 2019).  

PAH pollution directly and indirectly impacts human health and the well-being of other organisms globally (García-Sánchez et al., 2018). Selecting appropriate remediation strategies is critical, as these rely on factors such as the polluted matrix and prevailing environmental conditions (Kuppusamy et al., 2017). Methods involving physical, chemical, biological, and integrated approaches have been applied with varying degrees of success. Microbial-based techniques for ecological restoration of PAH-polluted environments have shown notable potential (Kuppusamy et al., 2017; Malla et al., 2018; Mehetre et al., 2019). Recently, integrated PAH remediation approaches have demonstrated effective pollutant mitigation. The aim of this review is to examine current knowledge and advancements in PAH remediation strategies, their associated factors, and their effectiveness or limitations. The review systematically outlines PAH characteristics, sources, exposure pathways, toxicity, health impacts, and the significance of remediation. Moreover, it highlights the contributions of -omics technologies and the constraints in PAH bioremediation.

### POLYCYCLIC AROMATIC HYDROCARBONS: PHYSICOCHEMICAL PROPERTIES, SOURCES OF POLLUTION, AND ROUTES OF EXPOSURE  

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons are organic pollutants composed of two or more fused aromatic rings made up of carbon and hydrogen atoms, primarily existing as colorless, white, or pale yellow solids (Abdel-Shafy and Mansour, 2016; Suman et al., 2016). The spatial arrangements of these aromatic rings can include linear, angular, or clustered configurations (Abdel-Shafy and Mansour, 2016).
